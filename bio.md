Hi. I am a PhD candidate at the <mark style="background-color:pink;">[Information School in University of Washington](https://ischool.uw.edu/)</mark> advised by [Jevin D. West](https://jevinwest.org/). My research is funded by the <mark style="background-color:pink;">[Center for an Informed Public](https://www.cip.uw.edu/)</mark>, can be broadly classified into *[Computational Social Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_social_science)*, and narrowly classified into **"AI Governance"** and **"Science of Science and Innovation"**.

Part of my research uses large-scale datasets to study collaboration networks, reputation, geopolitics, and emerging technologies (=AI) in science and innovation. This has led to publications in [Nature Human Behavior](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02154-0), [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2402053122), [Nature Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-79863-5), [Nature Scientific Data](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-05526-3), and [Philosophy of Science](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/philosophy-of-science/article/are-widely-known-findings-easier-to-retract/39F91FF2BB17A5DD6D315302254EB531), amongst others. I try to also force myself to think about what future infrastructure of science would/should look like. Can we create anticipatory governance for them? Science of science (my home field) is mostly a retrospective field; can we add a proactive lens, perhaps using Fiction, a ["Fiction Science of Science"](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7486114164588019712/) (FiSciSci: yes, I am indeed very proud of that acronym)?

More recently, I have been exploring how AI is becoming part of scientific practice: how AI tools affect scientific discovery, shape ideas of authorship and expertise, citations, peer review, and if AI agents can responsibly participate in research. I think of this emerging area as the "Science of Machine (-mediated) Science."

Part of my work also focuses on the AI governance itself: how countries, states, and organizations are establishing norms, rules, and policies around AI use, evaluation, development, and deployment. Some early work on technical AI governance has been published in [ICML (AI Welfare is Bullshit)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/xiao26x.html) and [CHI HEAL Workshop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13947). Increasingly, I am interested in institutional AI governance in the higher education context, studying how states, schools, and universities are governing AI, who is benefiting and who is being left behind? I have also served as a member of AI advisory group at Washington's Office of Superintendent of Public Instruction (OSPI), and coauthored [Washington State's AI Guidance for K-12 Public Schools](https://ospi.k12.wa.us/sites/default/files/2024-06/ai-guidance_ethics.pdf) in 2024. This guidance document has (by 2026) been cited and adopted by 11 other state guidances across the United States (see the citation network below from our working paper on studying state education policy guidelines).
